Strategic context

Fleury Group acquired Hermes Pardini in April 2023 for R$2.4 billion. Digital transformation — including the app redesign — was cited as a key catalyst in the synergy realization plan (R$220M+ annually). The app wasn't just a product improvement. It was part of a merger thesis.

02 — the app

One app. Every health decision, simplified.

Brazil's 3rd-largest diagnostic laboratory serves thousands of patients daily across São Paulo and Belo Horizonte. The app is where patients schedule exams, access results, and manage vaccinations — without visiting a unit or waiting on hold.

FEATURE_01

Exam scheduling

Book laboratory and imaging tests directly from the app. Geolocation finds the nearest unit with available slots and compatible services.

FEATURE_02

Real-time results

Push notification when results are released. Instant access to clinical analyses, imaging reports, and pathology results — on the patient's timeline, not the lab's.

FEATURE_03

Vaccination management

Full vaccination record tracking. Request mobile vaccination services at home. Never miss a booster — the app knows your schedule.

FEATURE_04

Physician tools

Dedicated Pardini Doctors experience: faster report visualization, critical result alerts, patient data synchronization across units.

FEATURE_05

Test information library

Comprehensive details on every test — preparation instructions, reference ranges, what the results mean. Patients arrive informed.

FEATURE_06

Biometric security

Face ID / Touch ID with secure keychain. No forced re-login per session. Healthcare data needs both accessibility and privacy — we built both.

04 — the problem

2.2 stars. Every review told the same story.

The app existed. Patients could technically use it. But the experience created friction at exactly the wrong moments — when someone needed their blood work results, or had to reschedule an appointment under time pressure.

FRICTION_01

No biometric auth

App required full password entry every session. No Face ID, no Touch ID, no remembered credentials. Patients accessing urgent results had to stop and type their password first.

FRICTION_02

Slow result loading

Physicians using Pardini Doctors reported unacceptable load times for patient data. In a clinical context, slow means dangerous.

FRICTION_03

Visual rendering bugs

Logo distortion on iPhone XR and similar devices — a trust signal problem at a company whose brand depends on precision.

FRICTION_04

Complex navigation

Routine tasks — scheduling and result viewing — required too many steps. The most frequent actions were the least accessible ones.

05 — design decisions

Five decisions that changed the experience

DEC_01

Biometric-first authentication

Face ID / Touch ID with secure keychain. Session persistence without compromising HIPAA-grade privacy. The most-used action in the app became the fastest one.

DEC_02

Information architecture rebuild

Simplified onboarding. Clearer CTA hierarchy. Results and scheduling surfaced to first-screen access. Measured by time-to-task across 6 core flows.

DEC_03

Healthcare design system

Space Mono for clinical data. Trust-focused color system (WCAG AA+). High-contrast critical value states. Modular component library for consistency across patient and physician experiences.

DEC_04

Performance + rendering fixes

Optimised API calls for result retrieval. Fixed logo rendering across all device types. Physician data load times reduced. Speed in healthcare is UX — and safety.

DEC_05

Personalisation layer

Custom notification preferences (results, exam reminders, vaccination alerts). Saved locations. Health history dashboard. The app learns the patient's routine instead of forcing them to re-configure every session.
